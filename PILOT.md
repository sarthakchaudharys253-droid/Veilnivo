# Veilnivo early pilot

Recruit 20 consenting freelancers, creators or online collaborators who regularly meet new contacts and prefer not to share phone numbers. Invite participants yourself; the application does not automatically contact anyone or collect analytics.

## Tasks

1. Create an alias and password without assistance.
2. Invite one other participant by single-use link/QR, or send a username request and accept it.
3. Exchange two messages, use a reply and reaction, and send a small encrypted file.
4. Set a timer, compare safety numbers through another channel and verify the identity.
5. Lock, sign in and recover history. Observe the clear difference between locking and erasing.
6. Try a connection interruption and inspect queued/retry states.
7. In a disposable workspace only, erase and check local/server outcomes.
8. Open Settings → Plans & feedback. Export feedback only if the participant chooses to share it.

## Record with consent

- Invites opened, identities created, accepted contacts and completed two-way conversations.
- Time and assistance needed to reach the first two-way conversation.
- Send/attachment failures and successful retries.
- Whether a participant voluntarily returns within a week.
- What they currently use, the task for which Veilnivo helps, and whether they would pay the proposed $4/month.

Do not collect message contents, passwords, private keys, phone numbers, contact graphs or unconsented device telemetry. Prefer aggregate counts and short voluntary notes. Keep billing research separate from pseudonymous messaging identities.

## Decisions

Look for a repeated useful task and voluntary repeat usage. Treat thresholds as hypotheses, not universal market benchmarks. Fix the largest first-conversation obstacle before expanding into groups, calls or enterprise administration. Paid AI requires separate cost/pricing validation and a real provider agreement.

The local preview is suitable for product usability feedback, not claims of security certification. A public security-sensitive launch needs a reviewed protocol and production database/device lifecycle verification.
